The Second Law of Financial Markets: Inertia

1. What Is Inertia?

Inertia is people's herding mentality — the herd effect. When one sheep jumps off a cliff, the rest follow. No one escapes the herd effect; if you think you have, it's only self-deception. You came to this market precisely because the herd effect is at work — think about it.

In the market, the herd effect is why trends continue. This market is made of people. Imagine if the market were full of quantitative bots with no emotions — what would the K-line chart look like? Answer: total chaos, and no one could make money. Precisely because people participate, and people are driven by hope, fear, greed, and ignorance, human behavior and reactions never change. So the market — past, present, and future — never changes.

You always want to predict the future, always clinging to the subconscious desire for certainty. That's because you haven't grasped the underlying logic and cognition — you can't see what the market is telling you through the fog. The four laws are your lighthouse in the fog, pointing out the direction. What we just did was analyze the present — how the market is moving now — and through the laws, you roughly know what may come next. We are not predicting.

It's like: if my sleep schedule is irregular, you can't guess that I'll get sick tomorrow, nor that I definitely will. You can only say I'll probably get sick. You say that because you've seen and heard what happens to people with irregular sleep — that's law and cognition.

All laws are statistical. Many don't understand this. I tell you rock-paper-scissors has a 50% win rate; you go lose 10 times in a row and say I lied. You simply don't get that laws are statistical. Without this cognition, you'll keep tinkering with your method, demanding perfection and certainty. If you can't patiently wait for probability to swing back your way, your job is simply to make sure you can keep playing after losing 10 times in a row — nothing more.

3. Inertia Never Disappears Into Thin Air

Yes, you're wondering: does inertia always continue? Obviously not. Inertia cannot suddenly vanish — but it can gradually disappear.

1. As time passes, inertia gradually fades.
2. As the price structure becomes complex, inertia gradually fades.

Case 1: Think of a car moving fast — when you hit the brakes, it doesn't stop instantly; it slows down gradually, no matter how hard you brake. Inertia fades over time.

Case 2: Same car analogy — if the road has many obstacles creating friction, the car stops faster. As the price structure becomes complex, it means the dominant force has hit obstacles, and inertia fades with the complexity.

When this happens, the only thing to do is exit immediately. If it continues up afterward, that's fine — we re-enter. Remember: time is the most expensive cost. We must minimize our costs to make the business worthwhile.
