The Third Law of Financial Markets: Regression

1. What Is Regression?

Regression studies the second leg — the correction. No matter how long you've traded, you may never have carefully thought about why regression occurs or how to use it. Regression is inevitable; only its form differs. Fundamentally, regression happens because of people — someone always takes profits emotionally.

2. Forms of Regression

There are two forms: 1. Time Regression   2. Price Regression.

Combined with last lesson's inertia, you should also realize that regression may cause inertia to fade or a new dominant force to emerge. Strong inertia matches strong regression — they are paired. So all four laws must be considered together.

Time Regression

What does time regression mean? After price rises or falls to a certain extent, it stops — neither up nor down, just moving sideways in a narrow range — before resuming the dominant force. You can also call it consolidation after strength.

Livermore's so-called "breakout" is exactly this technique. If you've read his books, you'll find the assets he went long or short on were always those already clearly in a trend. His goal was to stand on the market's correct side — not to fish in rain puddles. How to spot such assets was taught last lesson; it's not hard at all. A technique from 100 years ago still works today — that's a law.

This pattern signals that the up or down dominant force is very strong — because inertia is strong, price can't fall.

But combined with last lesson: inertia may gradually fade. If the time adjustment drags on too long, or 3–4 days after entering you still haven't profited — you should exit and observe. Why can't you learn others' techniques and still lose? Because you lack the cognition they have.

Price Regression

What is price regression? It's what we usually call a price correction. This kind of regression indicates the move is weakening. It can easily go too deep, causing the dominant force to gradually disappear.

3. Regression Produces Different Market Rhythms

What is rhythm? It refers to a process with a certain pattern. After you learn the next law, you'll understand the importance of rhythm. For now, recognize and feel the market's rhythm — it's like listening to music. Rhythm gives birth to patterns and repetition. Find the rhythm, and you'll find your entry point.

Rhythm Type 1: Standard Rhythm

Observe how the first "N" moves? The second "N" will likely move the same way — that's rhythm. Identifying and following the rhythm is your job. If you say you're dancing with the market but your rhythm and song don't match, how can you dance together? After learning the law of repetition, you'll know why the market creates such patterns. For now, just recognize and distinguish rhythms.

Note: Now that you understand rhythm, wouldn't you dare enter at the red dot?

Rhythm Type 2: Gentle Rhythm

In a gentle rhythm, the move from A to B is basically a straight line — no sharp spikes or drops. The market moves like an old lady: two steps forward, one adjustment, two steps forward, one adjustment.

Rhythm Type 3: Robust Rhythm

This rhythm is very common — the up and down moves resemble climbing up and down stairs.

Rhythm Type 4: Passionate Rhythm

Price moves from A to B very quickly, accompanied by only minor regression.

Rhythm Type 5: Rock Rhythm

This type is actually the hardest to trade — sharp rallies followed by sharp drops leave you unable to identify the dominant force.
