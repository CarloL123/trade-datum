The Fourth Law of Financial Markets: Repetition

Repetition elevates trading to the level of art. Trading is an art, not a technique.

If you observe carefully in life, you'll find repetition everywhere. With a bit of aesthetic sense, you know there's a beauty called the beauty of repetition — this beauty brings order and stability.

What repeats in the market is human nature. The chart reflects endlessly repeating human nature.

Last lesson we learned the market has six rhythm types. Rhythm is called "rhythm" precisely because it contains the beauty of repetition — otherwise it would just be noise. Laws give birth to beauty.

What repetition can you catch in the market?

A random screenshot from the software — observe these two lines: aren't their decline magnitudes roughly similar, K-line counts similar, and rhythm similar? That's repetition.

The hardest law to express rationally — this is an emotional cognition, which is why science students often fail at trading. Look at these three lines: aren't they nearly identical? But if you actually try to count whether every segment has the exact same K-line count, you'll never master this law. You must feel it.

Let me give you an example. Say you enter short right after a high appears, with your stop-loss above the high. Where do you place your take-profit? Here you can use repetition: place your take-profit at a length equal to the previous down-leg.

Whatever the magnitude of the first down-leg, that's your take-profit target.

The first box is the first down-leg's magnitude; the second box roughly replicates the first. When price moves from the box's high to its low, take profit.

Of course, I drew two boxes only to help you understand. In practice, just glance at the two lines and compare whether their lengths are roughly the same. Exit when the second leg looks similar to the first.

This is emotional cognition. Many people nitpick for an exact number or perfect match. You must understand: numbers are precise, but laws are fuzzy. We seek fuzziness, not precision — because precision doesn't exist in the market, or quant bots would take all the money.

This is the stair-step pattern we mentioned yesterday. It produces this so-called rhythm because of repetition. With cognition of rhythm and repetition, you can dance with the market instead of being half a beat behind.

When the market enters the box, the stair-step pattern is confirmed — because previously, price dropped after completing the platform. Can't you then go short anywhere inside the box? And the take-profit? Exit when the move roughly matches the previous leg's magnitude.

I know you're wondering how to short at the exact highest point in the box. I'd say: you don't need to. Doing that only makes you look like a twisted pervert — it won't bring you victory.

But with the law of repetition, what can you do? Answer: the previous platform's magnitude (from low to high) will repeat with a similar shape and magnitude in this platform.

Notice? The two boxes' magnitudes are nearly identical. But again — this is emotional cognition. Chasing absolute precision will leave you paralyzed, make you act like a paranoid in the market, and drive you mad.
