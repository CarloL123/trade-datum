Build Your Trading System

After learning the four laws, what you need now is to put the knowledge you've mastered to use and make money in the market. To earn money consistently in the market, you still need this final lesson. Laws exist at the human and emotional level, while a trading system operates at the mathematical and rational level. Only by combining rationality with emotion can you become an independent trader in the market.

The Impossible Trinity

Let's start with a concept from economics: the Impossible Trinity.

The Impossible Trinity describes the dilemma in choosing economic and financial policy goals — you cannot achieve all three objectives at once. In monetary policy, capital mobility, fixed exchange rates, and monetary independence cannot coexist.

That is the textbook definition. In the market, our Impossible Trinity is: Win Rate, Frequency, and Risk-Reward Ratio. You cannot have all three simultaneously.

If you choose Win Rate + Risk-Reward Ratio, you give up Frequency. You might only take one or two trades a year, but each is highly profitable. Does this fit your expectations and personality? That is for you to decide.

If you choose Win Rate + Frequency, you give up Risk-Reward Ratio. This means you must trade frequently to accumulate small profits into larger ones. Does this fit your expectations and personality? That is for you to decide.

If you choose Risk-Reward Ratio + Frequency, you give up Win Rate. You might be wrong 7 or 8 times and right only 2 or 3, but each winner is large enough to cover the losses. Does this fit your expectations and personality? That is for you to decide.

This lesson is called Build Your Trading System — not mine, not anyone else's. You must choose based on your own personality and preferences. No choice is inherently better; only which suits you best. Every choice can lead to big profits.

You keep changing your methods and strategies because you don't truly understand the Impossible Trinity. You hope to find a method that gives you all three — but that perfection doesn't exist in reality or in the market. You must give up one. Which will it be? Only you can answer that.

Many people ask me: "I keep losing on false breakouts in ranging markets — how do I avoid this?" My answer: trade on longer timeframes and abandon frequency. But most people don't understand what I'm saying; I'm playing the lute to a cow. They don't know their own choice, and their trading method seems picked up from nowhere like scavenging trash. Frequent losses simply mean you chose Frequency + Risk-Reward Ratio, profiting from a large risk-reward ratio — what you gave up is Win Rate.

From now on, you must be clear about what you want and what you don't. Don't ask me these questions — if you can't figure out what you want yourself, what can I do?

Fix Your Risk-Reward Ratio First

Fix your risk-reward ratio first, then choose Win Rate or Frequency. For example: I fix my risk-reward ratio at 3:1. From then on, in every trade, my take-profit must be 3 times my stop-loss. If it's not 3:1, I don't enter. With a 3:1 ratio, choosing Frequency means I can only trade short-term moves — longer timeframes won't satisfy my frequency needs, and Win Rate is what I give up. With this choice, I won't fear frequent stop-losses, because a low win rate is my own decision — I just keep trading.

Reference ratios:

• 1.5:1 risk-reward ratio → 50% win rate is enough for consistent profits.

• 3:1 risk-reward ratio → 30% win rate is enough for consistent profits.

• 100:1 risk-reward ratio → even random entries can profit consistently.

• 1:100 risk-reward ratio → random entries yield a win rate near 100%, also profitable.

Consistency

You must stick to what you want and what you don't. You must remain consistent. If today you choose Win Rate + Risk-Reward Ratio, tomorrow Frequency + Risk-Reward Ratio, and the day after something else — that won't work. You're still chasing perfection. So once you decide, don't change it for at least half a year. This is consistency: don't waver, be unwavering.

Consistency also means: once you set a trading strategy, you cannot change it while holding a position. Decide in advance when to enter, when to exit, and your reason for going long or short. Trust your judgment; don't fear being wrong. From now on, before placing any order, you must write down the answers to these three questions — you must write them.
