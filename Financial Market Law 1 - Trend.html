Trend

I've talked about trends with many people and found that most only half-understand it, taking it casually. We need to rigorously and objectively analyze what trend exists at the timeframe you want to trade. That is the goal of this first lesson.

1. Three Types of Trends

Trends come in three forms: Uptrend, Downtrend, and Sideways (Range). Anyone who has traded for over a month probably knows these three — yet 90% of traders, even those with 10+ years of experience, have never seriously studied this concept.

• Uptrend: Higher lows and higher highs.
• Downtrend: Lower lows and lower highs.
• Sideways: Anything that is not an uptrend or downtrend.

Remember: there are three types of trends — three! Sideways is also a trend. Don't treat ranging markets as something to avoid. After reading this document, think: how would you handle a ranging market?

Why Trend Is an Inevitable Law

Why do I say trend is one of the unavoidable laws of financial markets? Because supply and demand — the fundamental force driving price from point A to point B — always moves in trends.

Note that a move from A to B requires a certain magnitude. For example, a move from $1 to $1.50 is largely random — some careless trader might have hit the wrong button. But a move from $1 to $10 cannot be manipulated by anyone. Even if market makers push price from $1 to $10, they can't make it go straight up; some will take profits, making pullbacks and corrections inevitable. This is human nature, this is the law, this is certainty.

2. Trends Have Timeframes

Now that you understand the definition of trend, you need to know that trends operate at different timeframes.

From now on, use only one time window when looking at charts. Pick whichever you like, but once decided, don't randomly switch to other timeframes. Switching makes your judgment ambiguous and causes you to lose direction. We'll discuss more once you've reached a higher level.

You can draw however you like. If you want short-term trades, observe smaller-timeframe trends; if you want bigger moves, observe larger-timeframe trends. It's your control, your preference — whichever fits your personality.

Do you notice? Large timeframes contain smaller ones. So is this an uptrend or a downtrend? It looks like both — the leftmost low is higher than the rightmost low. The logic breaks down, and your reading becomes ambiguous — ambiguity leaves you unsure how to act. So first decide which timeframe you want to trade, then draw your lines.

The purpose of timeframes is to help you find direction — so you don't get lost in the K-line chart.

• For short trades → observe the current small-timeframe trend.
• For medium trades → observe the current medium-timeframe trend.
• For larger trades → observe the current large-timeframe trend.

Keep it simple. Don't complicate things by watching both large and small timeframes at once. You'll easily think you're trading a large timeframe when you're actually in a small one, or vice versa. It's not that you can't do it — you just don't have the skill yet. You need more observation and practice first. One step at a time.

So I require you to observe only ONE timeframe. First decide your timeframe, then analyze what trend is currently in play on it.

The only thing you must master in this lesson: Decide your timeframe first, then analyze the current trend on that timeframe. Just two things: timeframe and trend.
